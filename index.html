    3 · Damping

    Optimal, over-damped and under-damped

    The catheter, tubing and transducer behave like a mass on a spring. Two properties decide how faithfully they reproduce the pressure: the natural frequency (how fast the system can oscillate) and the damping coefficient (how quickly oscillations die out). Each tracing below shows a fast flush followed by the patient's waveform through that system.

    Optimal

    Flush test
    Sharp square wave, then 1–2 oscillations that settle quickly to the waveform.
    Waveform
    Crisp upstroke, rounded peak, distinct dicrotic notch.
    Numbers
    SBP, DBP and MAP all accurate.
    Typical values
    ζ about 0.5–0.7 with fn above about 15–20 Hz.

    Over-damped

    Flush test
    Slow, sloped return to baseline with fewer than 1.5 oscillations, often none.
    Waveform
    Flattened and blunted. Slurred upstroke, dicrotic notch lost, narrow pulse pressure.
    Numbers
    SBP falsely low, DBP falsely high. MAP stays close to true.
    Causes
    • Air bubbles in the line or transducer (most common)
    • Clot at the catheter tip
    • Kinked catheter or tubing; wrist position
    • Catheter tip against the vessel wall
    • Compliant (non-pressure) tubing, loose connections
    • Flush bag under 300 mmHg
    Fix
    Aspirate and flush, remove air, reposition the wrist, check connections and bag pressure.

    Under-damped

    Flush test
    Ringing: more than 2–3 oscillations after the flush before it settles.
    Waveform
    Tall, narrow, spiky systolic peak ("overshoot" or "fling"), extra oscillations, exaggerated notch.
    Numbers
    SBP falsely high, DBP may read slightly low. MAP stays close to true.
    Causes
    • Long tubing (lowers fn)
    • Extra stopcocks and connections
    • Tachycardia or hyperdynamic circulation
    • Stiff, non-compliant arteries
    • Catheter whip
    Fix
    Shorten tubing, remove extra stopcocks, use a damping device; trust MAP over SBP until fixed.

    Calculating fn and ζ from the square wave

    This is the step response of whatever system is set on the monitor above. Move the ζ and fn sliders and the measurement updates.

    Oscillations after release--
    Peak-to-peak period--
    Measured natural frequency--
    Amplitude ratio A2 / A1--
    Calculated ζ--
    Interpretation--
    fn = 1 ÷ period (s)
    At 25 mm/s paper speed: fn = 25 ÷ distance between peaks (mm)
    ζ = −ln(A2/A1) ÷ √(π² + ln²(A2/A1))

    A1 and A2 are the heights of two successive oscillations measured from the settled baseline. A higher fn tolerates a wider range of ζ; a low fn (long tubing) leaves almost no acceptable damping range.
